What MeritNama does

One place to make sense of postgraduate induction.

Official merit lists and gazettes are long, scattered and hard to compare across rounds. MeritNama organises them so you can reason about your options.

Merit analysis

Apply the published scoring policy for your induction to see how your merit is composed, component by component.

Historical cutoffs

Closing merit by specialty, hospital, programme and quota across past inductions and rounds — normalised so different scoring scales can be compared.

Seat competition

Seat counts from official gazettes alongside how many candidates competed for them, so you can see where competition is concentrated.

Preference planning

Build and compare draft preference lists with historical context, quota filters and duplicate warnings before you submit officially.

Induction simulation

Run a merit-order seat-allocation simulation over the candidate pool to explore how preferences could play out, round by round.

How it works

From your marks to your options, in four steps.

  1. Calculate your merit

    Enter your marks and the calculator applies the scoring policy published for your induction — each component shown with its maximum, so nothing is hidden.

  2. Compare with historical cutoffs

    See where your score sits against closing merit for the specialties, hospitals and quotas you care about, with the trend across years.

  3. Plan your preferences

    Order specialties and hospitals into draft lists, check eligibility warnings and compare alternatives side by side.

  4. Simulate the induction

    Run the allocation simulation on the available data to see a predicted placement — and the preference that produced it. Every result is labelled as a simulation.

Historical merit & cutoffs

Read the history before you choose.

  • Every induction we hold data for — closing merit per specialty, hospital, programme (FCPS, MS, MD, MDS) and quota.
  • Round-by-round movement — how cutoffs shifted between merit lists within an induction.
  • Comparable scales — scoring totals changed between policies, so scores are also shown as a percentage of each year’s maximum.
Seat competition & simulation

See competition, not just cutoffs.

  • Seats from the gazette — the number of positions per specialty, hospital and quota.
  • Merit-order allocation — the simulator walks candidates in merit order and fills seats against stated preferences and quotas.
  • Unplaced outcomes shown honestly — when a list would not place, the simulation says so.
Data & methodology

Where the numbers come from.

MeritNama uses published induction material — merit lists, gazette seat notifications and scoring-policy notifications — processed into a consistent format. Each induction cycle the data is updated as new lists are published.

  • Sources: official merit lists and gazette notifications for each induction and round; the scoring policy notification for each induction.
  • Processing: hospital and specialty names are normalised to canonical identifiers so they can be compared across years.
  • Corrections: if you spot a mismatch with an official document, tell us and we will check it against the source.

How figures are labelled

Historical

Taken from a published merit list or gazette for a completed round.

Current

From the induction in progress. May change as further rounds and corrections are published.

Simulated

Produced by MeritNama’s model from available data and stated assumptions. Never an official selection decision.

Always verify officially

Historical cutoffs do not guarantee future outcomes, and simulations depend on incomplete information. Confirm everything with the official induction authority before you decide.

FAQ

Questions trainees ask us.

Is MeritNama an official induction or admissions portal?

No. MeritNama is independent and is not affiliated with PHF, CPSP, the Health Department or any admissions authority. Applications, consent and official results happen only through the official channels.

Does a simulated placement mean I will get that seat?

No. Simulations use the data available to MeritNama and stated assumptions. Real outcomes depend on official lists, consent, joining, withdrawals and corrections that a simulation cannot know in advance.

Where does the data come from?

From published merit lists, gazette seat notifications and scoring-policy notifications for each induction. See Data & methodology.

Can I change my marks in MeritNama?

Your published record cannot be edited by you. You can enter your own figures in the calculator to explore “what if” scenarios — those are clearly marked as your own input, separate from published data.

Who can see my saved preference lists and scenarios?

Only your account. MeritNama does not publish individual candidates’ plans. Read the privacy policy for details.

I found an error. How do I report it?

Email itskaero@gmail.com with the induction, round and a link or reference to the official document. We check every report against the source.
